Drop photos or a folder · 2–8 JPEG, PNG or Sony ARW of one scene

      What to expect

      The output is exactly twice the width and height of your photos. Merging cuts noise and cleans up fine detail a little. It does not give you four times the detail. In our tests the gain over upscaling one JPEG was about 0.7–1.3 dB PSNR. In simulations, RAW files of the same burst scored 0.9–2.5 dB better than camera JPEGs.

      The scene must hold still. Shifts, slight rotation and handheld wobble are corrected; places where something moved are left out of the merge for that photo. Camera-shake blur inside a photo is not fixed.

      How to shoot a burst

      1. Pick a still scene: a landscape, a building, a product on a table.
      2. Brace the camera. A tripod is best. Handheld works if you hold steady; the app corrects small movements.
      3. Lock the settings: manual exposure, the same ISO, shutter speed and focus for every frame.
      4. Shoot 4 to 8 frames in continuous mode. Tiny movements between frames are what add detail.
      5. Drop the photos here. The app picks the sharpest one as the reference.
